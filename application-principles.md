# Application Principles

Reusable convictions to thread through cover letters and interview responses. Written in first person -- adapt framing per role, but don't soften.

---

## 1. The org chart doesn't survive this

AI doesn't slot into existing functions, it redraws them. Customer service, ops, legal review, sales engineering -- these aren't roles getting tools, they're categories getting rewritten. Telling a buyer their team structure stays intact to make the sale easier is how a competitor gazumps you in 12 months. Honesty is harder. It's also shorter, and it's the one that produces real expansion.

**Use when:** The role involves enterprise sales, go-to-market, transformation leadership, or advisory. Especially where the hiring company's customers are large incumbents.

---

## 2. Pilots designed to succeed are theatre

Most enterprise AI pilots are scoped to produce a positive case study rather than to test whether the workflow actually changes. A pilot that craters with real learnings beats ten that quietly hit their KPI. The customers who scale AI fastest treat their first deployment as a falsifiable experiment, not a reference call in waiting. They write the kill criteria before the success criteria. They commit to telling the executive sponsor the uncomfortable answer if that's where the data went.

**Use when:** The role involves delivery, pre-sales, consulting, or product management. Anywhere "proof of concept" or "pilot" appears in the JD.

---

## 3. AI adoption is not a cloud migration

The cloud playbook assumes the workflow stays the same and the infrastructure changes underneath. AI inverts that. The buyers you don't want are the ones treating this like the last platform shift. You refuse that frame in meeting one, or you don't refuse it at all.

**Use when:** The role involves strategy, architecture, or platform leadership. Especially at consulting firms or vendors where migration-style thinking is the default sales motion.

---

## 4. Lead with the workflow, not the model

Customers don't buy capability, they buy a new operating shape. The pitch that lands names the work going away, not the work being augmented. The rest is demo theatre.

**Proof points:**
- Pendula: campaign teams collapsing six tools into one
- Billcap: an industry with learned helplessness about churn, finally given a tool that treated the customer as worth keeping

**Use when:** Any customer-facing or product role. The universal version of the Anthropic-specific closer.

---

## Closing frame

Frontier capability without workflow honesty is just better demos.
