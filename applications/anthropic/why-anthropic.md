Anthropic is the only company where I would be honest about what I will not do in this role. Four things, then.

I will not pretend the org chart survives this. AI doesn't slot into existing functions, it redraws them. Customer service, ops, legal review, sales engineering -- these aren't roles getting tools, they're categories getting rewritten. Telling a buyer their team structure stays intact to make the sale easier is how a competitor gazumps us in 12 months. Honesty is harder. It's also shorter, and it's the one that produces real expansion. The customers who get this move from pilot to platform in a single budget cycle. The ones who don't spend two years renewing a tool that nobody in the business can name a workflow for.

I will not run a pilot designed to succeed. Most enterprise AI pilots are theatre, scoped to produce a positive case study rather than to test whether the workflow actually changes. A pilot that craters with real learnings beats ten that quietly hit their KPI. The customers I've seen scale AI fastest treated their first deployment as a falsifiable experiment, not a reference call in waiting. They wrote the kill criteria before the success criteria. They committed to telling the executive sponsor the uncomfortable answer if that's where the data went. That posture is rare, and it's the one that produces customers worth having.

I will not run an AI adoption like a cloud migration. The cloud playbook assumes the workflow stays the same and the infrastructure changes underneath. AI inverts that. The buyers you probably don't want in an inference-constrained world are the ones treating this like the last platform shift. You refuse that frame in meeting one, or you don't refuse it at all.

I will not lead with the model. Customers don't buy capability, they buy a new operating shape. At Pendula that was campaign teams collapsing six tools into one. At Billcap it was an industry with learned helplessness about churn and low customer trust, finally given a tool that treated the customer as worth keeping. The pitch that lands names the work going away, not the work being augmented. The rest is demo theatre.

Frontier capability without workflow honesty is just better demos. The reason I want this seat is that the technical presales motion at Anthropic is the one place that conversation can be had at scale, with the model behind it that makes the conversation worth having.
